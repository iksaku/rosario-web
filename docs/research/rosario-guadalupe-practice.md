## 1. Full sequence of the Rosary in the Guadalupe/Mexican tradition vs the generic Latin-rite Rosary

### 1.1 What "El Rosario a la Virgen de Guadalupe" is in Mexico

In Mexico the Rosary prayed "a la Virgen de Guadalupe" is overwhelmingly the standard Latin-rite Dominican Rosary — same 20 mysteries, same weekday cycle, same decade anatomy — celebrated with Guadalupe-specific **openings, intentions, jaculatorias, songs, and feast-day cadence**, not a different set of mysteries. The Arquidiócesis de México's own official guides model exactly this: the annual Pilgrimage Rosary to the Basilica prays the **five Joyful Mysteries** with a Guadalupe opening (invocation, prayer for the local Church, Act of Contrition, Ven Espíritu Santo) and closes with the Guadalupe jaculatoria "Santa María de Guadalupe, Reina de México / Salva nuestra patria y conserva nuestra fe" after each decade [APM-PDF]. A separate "Rosario Guadalupano" with five mysteries drawn from the Tepeyac apparitions (offered on the 12th of each month at the Basilica's *Rosario del Encuentro Guadalupano*) exists as a complementary devotion [BAS], but it is an extra devotion, not a replacement for the daily Rosary.

### 1.2 The generic Latin-rite Rosary (Vatican baseline)

Per the Holy See's own summary: Sign of the Cross → optional opening (the Vatican's schema uses "Dios mío, ven en mi auxilio… / Gloria") → announcement of each mystery → **brief pause for reflection** → one *Padrenuestro*, ten *Avemarías*, one *Gloria* → an invocation/jaculatoria may be added after each decade → at the end the *Letanía Lauretana* "u otras oraciones marianas" [VAT-ROS]. *Rosarium Virginis Mariae* §31 recommends a brief pause after listening to the Word (a Bible verse announcing the mystery), §34–35 treat the *Gloria* and the closing jaculatoria, and §37 endorses the *Salve Regina* or the Lorentine Litanies as the crowning closing prayer; an "oración final" is legitimate and varies by tradition [VAT-RVM].

### 1.3 The sequence as prayed in Mexico (composite of the official APM guides)

**Opening (Oraciones introductorias)** — Guadalupe-specific in flavor:

1. **Señal de la Cruz** — with the Mexican full form: "Por la señal de la Santa Cruz, de nuestros enemigos líbranos Señor, Dios nuestro. En el nombre del Padre, y del Hijo, y del Espíritu Santo. Amén." [DLF-R]
2. **Acto de Contrición** — "Señor mío Jesucristo, Dios y hombre verdadero…" prayed aloud by all [APM-PDF; DLF-R uses the *Yo confieso* variant].
3. Optional invocation to the Holy Spirit ("Ven Espíritu Santo, llena los corazones de tus fieles…") in solemn parish/pilgrimage use [APM-PDF].

**Five decades**, each:

4. **Announcement of the mystery** with a Bible reading (in group use a *lector* proclaims the Gospel passage; "Todos oran un momento en silencio; después, el guía continúa" [APM-RV]) — this implements RVM §29–31 (announcement opens a "scene", brief pause of silence follows).
5. Brief reflection/offering of the mystery for a concrete intention (peace, migrants, families — in the APM guide each mystery is "offered" for an intention of the Mexican Church) [APM-PDF; APM-RV].
6. **Padrenuestro** (the Guía/leader intones: "Padre nuestro que estás en el cielo…" and all continue) — then **10 Avemarías**, then **Gloria** [APM-PDF; DLF-R].
7. **Jaculatoria after the Gloria** — the Archdiocese uses the Guadalupe versicle and response: **V. "Santa María de Guadalupe, Reina de México" / R. "Salva nuestra patria y conserva nuestra fe."** [APM-PDF]. Family/parish guides more often print the classic pair: "María, Madre de gracia, Madre de misericordia…" and "Oh Jesús mío, perdona nuestros pecados, líbranos del fuego del infierno…" plus "Sagrado Corazón de Jesús, en vos confío" [DLF-R].
8. **A song (canto)** after each decade in sung group use — e.g. *Santa María del Camino*, *Niña*, *Jesús, ¿quién eres tú?* [APM-PDF].

**Closing (Oraciones finales)** — this is where the Mexican custom is most distinctive [DLF-R; APM-PDF]:

9. **Padrenuestro, Avemaría y Gloria "por las intenciones del Santo Padre"** (the Pope's intentions — this is the indulgence clause: praying one Padrenuestro, 3 Avemarías and the Salve for the Pope's intentions on the day the Rosary is prayed in a church or oratory, or in family [APM-PDF explicitly notes "Para ganar la indulgencia plenaria, … ofrecerá el padrenuestro, las 3 aves marías y la Salve, por las intenciones del Santo Padre"]).
10. **The three "Avemarías de la fe, la esperanza y la caridad"** — "Dios te salve, María Santísima, Hija de Dios Padre, Virgen Purísima antes del parto, en tus manos encomiendo mi fe…" / "…Madre de Dios Hijo, Virgen Purísima en el parto, en tus manos encomiendo mi esperanza…" / "…esposa de Dios Espíritu Santo, Virgen Purísima después del parto, en tus manos encomiendo mi caridad…" — then the fourth: "Dios te salve, María Santísima, Templo, trono y sagrario de la Santísima Trinidad, Virgen concebida sin la culpa original. Dios te salve…" [DLF-R].
11. **La Salve** ("Dios te salve, Reina y Madre de misericordia…") followed by the versicle "Ruega por nosotros, Santa Madre de Dios / para que seamos dignos de alcanzar las promesas de Nuestro Señor Jesucristo" [DLF-R; VAT-ROS].
12. **Letanías Lauretanas** (see §5.2 for textual notes) [DLF-R; APM-PDF; VAT-LIT].
13. **Oración final** — typically the collect "Oh Dios, cuyo Unigénito Hijo, con su vida, muerte y resurrección, nos alcanzó el premio de la vida eterna: concédenos, a los que recordamos estos misterios del Santo Rosario, imitar lo que contienen y alcanzar lo que prometen…" [site text, matching the Roman collect; cf. VAT-RVM §37 which quotes its Latin parent, "imitar lo que contienen y conseguir lo que prometen"].

### 1.4 Summary table: Mexico vs generic Latin rite

| Element | Generic Latin-rite Rosary (Vatican schema) | Mexican / Guadalupe practice |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Sign of Cross + "Dios mío, ven en mi auxilio" or Credo | Señal de la Cruz (full Mexican form) + **Acto de Contrición** (+ *Yo confieso* or *Ven Espíritu Santo* in solemn use) |
| Mysteries | 20 in 4 sets; weekday cycle (RVM §38) | Same 20 mysteries, same cycle — no separate Guadalupe cycle for the daily Rosary |
| Decade anatomy | mystery → pause → Padr Nuestro + 10 Avemarías + Gloria (+ jaculatoria) | Identical, plus: Bible reading by a lector, **offering of the mystery for an intention**, Guadalupe jaculatoria, **song after the decade** |
| Post-decade jaculatoria | free/varies by custom (RVM §35) | "Santa María de Guadalupe, Reina de México / Salva nuestra patria y conserva nuestra fe" (APM official) or "María, Madre de gracia…" + "Oh Jesús mío…" (family use) |
| Closing | Salve and/or Letanías lauretanas + free oración final (RVM §37) | **Prayers for the Pope's intentions (Padre Nuestro + Avemaría + Gloria)** → **3 Avemarías fe/esperanza/caridad + 4th "Templo y Sagrario"** → **Salve** → **Letanías** → oración final; Cordero de Dios triptych often sung before the final collect |

## 2. Guía/Todos roles and solo prayer

### 2.1 The roles in group prayer

Mexican Rosary guides are printed as **liturgical dialogues**. The two printed roles are:

- **Guía** (guide/leader; the APM guides print **V.** / **R.** versicle-and-response, and name the function "el guía continúa"): announces each mystery, intoneses the Padrenuestro and the first half of versicles, gives the reflections, and leads the transitions [APM-PDF; APM-RV; DLF-R].
- **Todos** (all/everyone; printed **R.**): makes the responses, joins the second half of split prayers, prays the decades, and answers the litanies ("ruega por nosotros") [same sources].

In solemn archdiocesan use the roles multiply but stay dialogic: a *presidente* gives the opening message, a different priest or lay person **directs each mystery's reflection**, a **lector** proclaims the Gospel, a designated couple/young people/children lead the decade prayers and the jaculatoria, the cantor leads the song, and the litanies are sung "desde la cabina" [APM-PDF].

### 2.2 How this maps to solo use

Nothing in the structure changes when praying alone — the Church explicitly frames the Rosary as valid both personally and communally: RVM entrusts "la causa de la paz" to "su recitación comunitaria y su práctica constante" while §41 calls it "oración de la familia y por la familia", and the Vatican how-to schema addresses a single pray-er ("En el nombre del Padre… Dios mío, ven en mi auxilio") [VAT-RVM; VAT-ROS]. Practically, for a solo user of a Guía/Todos site:

- **Read every line aloud yourself**: the Guía/Todos labels describe *who speaks in a group*, not different content. A lone pray-er simply reads both parts sequentially — this is exactly how printed guides are used at home.
- The dialogue survives as **rhythm**, not as casting: the split of the Padrenuestro/Avemaría/Gloria into halves (leader: first half → all: second half) becomes a natural breathing/pacing device.
- The **silence after the announced mystery** (RVM §31: "es oportuno hacer una breve pausa después de escuchar la Palabra de Dios") is the one element that gains importance when alone — it replaces the group's shared pause and is the contemplative heart of each decade.
- Design implication: a solo-oriented UI can present Guía/Todos as typographic alternation (e.g. leader lines in one weight/color, responses in another) so the two roles remain visible without implying a second person is required.

## 3. Decade structure and weekday assignment of the mysteries

### 3.1 Anatomy of a decade

Canonical form, confirmed identically by the Vatican schema, the APM official guide, and the Desde la Fe weekly guides:

1. **Announcement** of the mystery (with its Bible verse where guides provide one) [VAT-ROS; APM-PDF; DLF-R].
2. **Brief pause of silence** for reflection — explicitly recommended by RVM §31 and printed as instruction in the APM guide ("Todos oran un momento en silencio; después, el guía continúa") [APM-RV].
3. **Un Padrenuestro** [all sources].
4. **Diez Avemarías** [all sources].
5. **Un Gloria** [all sources].
6. **Una jaculatoria** (RVM §35 says the jaculatoria after the doxology "varía según las costumbres"; Mexico: the Guadalupe versicle or the classic Fatima-style prayers) [VAT-RVM; APM-PDF; DLF-R].

### 3.2 The four sets and weekday assignment

The four-series scheme with weekday assignment was fixed by *Rosarium Virginis Mariae* §38 (2002), which moved Thursday's mysteries to make room for the Luminous Mysteries: gozosos Monday **and Saturday** (Saturday moved from the gloriosos because "el sábado es tradicionalmente un día de marcado carácter mariano"), luminosos **Thursday**, dolorosos Tuesday and Friday, gloriosos Wednesday and Sunday [VAT-RVM §38; VAT-ROS]. The assignment is explicitly non-binding: "esta indicación no pretende limitar una conveniente libertad en la meditación personal y comunitaria" [VAT-RVM §38].

| Day | Mysteries |
|---|---|
| Monday / Saturday | Gozosos (joyful) |
| Tuesday / Friday | Dolorosos (sorrowful) |
| Wednesday / Sunday | Gloriosos (glorious) |
| Thursday | Luminosos (luminous) |

The site's `Misterios.tsx` mapping (Gozosos Mon/Sat, Luminosos Thu, Dolorosos Tue/Fri, Gloriosos Wed/Sun) **matches the universal scheme exactly** — and therefore standard Mexican practice, since the Mexican guides follow the same RVM cycle [DLF-R: "Los Misterios Gozosos del Santo Rosario se rezan los lunes y sábado"].

### 3.3 Canonical mystery names (for comparison with the site)

From the Vatican's Spanish mystery pages [VAT-MIS-G/L/D/GL]:

- **Gozosos**: 1) La Encarnación del Hijo de Dios; 2) La Visitación de Nuestra Señora a su prima Santa Isabel; 3) El Nacimiento del Hijo de Dios en el portal de Belén; 4) La presentación de Jesús en el Templo; 5) El Niño Jesús perdido y hallado en el Templo.
- **Luminosos**: 1) El Bautismo en el Jordán; 2) Las bodas de Caná; 3) El anuncio del Reino de Dios; 4) La Transfiguración; 5) La institución de la Eucaristía.
- **Dolorosos**: 1) La oración en el Huerto; 2) La flagelación de Jesús atado a la columna; 3) La coronación de espinas; 4) Jesús con la Cruz a cuestas camino del Calvario;  5) La crucifixión y muerte de Jesús.
- **Gloriosos**: 1) La resurrección del Hijo de Dios; 2) La Ascensión del Señor al cielo; 3) La venida del Espíritu Santo; 4) La Asunción de María al cielo; 5) La coronación de María como Reina y Señora de todo lo creado.

## 4. Visual/imagery language of the Guadalupe tradition (for a respectful design system)

The Guadalupe image itself is described by Mexican Catholic sources as a **"códice"** — a painted document whose every element carries meaning [DLF-SYM]. Design elements with documented meaning:

### 4.1 Colors (with documented symbolic anchors)

- **Guadalupe blue-green (azul-verde / turquoise)** — the mantle (manto). "La cubre de pies a cabeza, indica que es una emperatriz… el azul representa el cielo y el verde la vida" [DLF-SYM]. This is *the* signature color of the tradition; any palette anchored on the tilma should keep the blue-green mantle as primary.
- **Rose / salmon-pink (rosa salmonado)** — the tunic (túnica), "con sombras entre marrón y carmesí, representa la tierra" [DLF-SYM]. Secondary anchor.
- **Gold (dorado)** — the rays (rayos/mandorla), the nine golden flowers of the tunic, the angel's medallion; divinity/light/the sun [DLF-SYM]. Use for accents, halos, and highlights — not large fields.
- **Black moon (luna negra)** — the crescent she stands on; also the black cross brooch and the black ribbon (cinta) of pregnancy [DLF-SYM]. Use sparingly, as contrast.
- **Green–white–red** — the angel's wings: "Los colores verde, blanco y rojo, sagrados para los indígenas y que más tarde se incorporaron a la bandera mexicana" [DLF-SYM]. A subtle nod available to the design system, but handle with care: these are also the national colors, and overuse would read as civic rather than devotional.

### 4.2 Symbols and motifs

- **La tilma** — the agave-fiber cloak of Juan Diego on which the image appeared; the decisive "sign" (Castilian roses carried in December, which fell when the tilma was opened before the bishop) [BAS, *Nican Mopohua* §§125–133, 181–184]. The tilma's texture (visible weave, "agujeros, costuras visibles, rasgaduras y nudos de los hilos" [DLF-SYM]) is a documented motif: a design system can echo the woven texture as a background/subtle pattern.
- **Roses (rosas)** — the flowers Juan Diego cut on the Tepeyac hillside as the requested sign; "nepapan caxtillan tlazoxochitl" (various precious Castilian roses) blooming out of season [BAS, NM §§125–133]. Motif for illustrations and section dividers.
- **The 46 stars** on the mantle, "coinciden con las constelaciones visibles sobre el Valle de México durante el solsticio de invierno de 1531" [DLF-SYM]. Star field as a decorative motif (progress indicators, background).
- **The sun rays / mandorla** surrounding her, "símbolo de una nueva y luminosa era", and the **moon** under her feet [DLF-SYM].
- **Nahui Ollin** — the four-petaled flower over the womb, symbol of "el Dios verdadero por quien se vive" [DLF-SYM; cf. NM §26 where Mary calls God "Ipalnemohuani (Aquel por quien se vive)"]. A distinctive geometric motif for the design system.
- **Hands joined in prayer** forming the "casita" she asked for; the **angel** uniting heaven (mantle) and earth (tunic) [DLF-SYM].
- **"¿No estoy yo aquí, que soy tu Madre?"** — the consoling words of the fourth apparition (NM §119: "¿Cuix amo nican nica nimonantzin…", "¿No estoy yo aquí, que soy tu Madre? ¿No estás bajo mi sombra y protección? ¿No soy yo la fuente de tu alegría? ¿No estás por ventura en mi regazo?") [BAS]. This is the most-quoted Guadalupe sentence in Mexican devotion; candidate for the site's emotional anchor copy (in Spanish, as is).

### 4.3 Calendar anchors

- **12 December** — feast of Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe; the Basilica fills with pilgrims and *Las Mañanitas* are sung at midnight [DLF general coverage]. The site could mark this date.
- **Day 12 of every month** — the Basilica's *Rosario del Encuentro Guadalupano* [BAS].
- **The weekly Saturday marian character** (RVM §38) and **October, month of the Rosary** (RVM proclaimed Oct 2002–Oct 2003 the *Año del Rosario*, and the APM pilgrimage guides date from January) [VAT-RVM; APM-PDF].

## 5. Deviations between the current site texts and standard practice (flag-only list)

> The site's texts are untouchable per the issue. This section only records, for the owner's awareness, where the current `src/` content differs from the primary-source texts cited above.

### 5.1 Sequence/structure flags

1. **The site has no decade loop.** `Misterios.tsx` lists the five mystery names as a static `<ul>`; the site never walks the pray-er through announcement → Padrenuestro → 10 Avemarías → Gloria → jaculatoria. The Mexican guides all center this loop [APM-PDF; DLF-R]. (This is the biggest gap between the site as "one long scroll of prayer texts" and the practice as prayed.)
2. **"Oración por las intenciones del Santo Padre" placement.** On the site this section comes immediately after the Misterios section and before the Jaculatorias — matching the position of the "prayers for the Pope's intentions" block in Mexican guides (which place them right after the five decades, before the 3 Avemarías and Salve) [DLF-R §"Oraciones finales" step 5–6; APM-PDF]. Placement is fine; the flag is that its **content** on the site is a full Padre Nuestro + Avemaría + Gloria as a Guía/Todos split (matching the indulgence requirement of "un Padrenuestro, 3 Avemarías y la Salve" only partially — it prints one Avemaría, not three, and the Salve appears only later inside the Jaculatorias section as the tail of the fourth "Dios te salve") [APM-PDF; DLF-R].
3. **The 3 Avemarías (fe/esperanza/caridad) are in the Jaculatorias section.** On the site they appear as items inside "Jaculatorias" after two classic jaculatorias; in the Mexican guides they form their own closing block ("Se rezan 3 Avemarías") together with the fourth "Templo y Sagrario de la Santísima Trinidad" Hail Mary and then the Salve [DLF-R]. Flag: the site's ordering works, but a redesign could group them as the distinct closing unit they are.
4. **No Salve as a standalone prayer.** The Salve arrives on the site only as the response tail of the fourth "Dios te salve, María Santísima, templo, trono y sagrario…" item. Mexican guides print **La Salve** as its own named prayer after the 3+1 Avemarías [DLF-R]. Flag for awareness.
5. **Letanías formatting**: the site renders each invocation as plain text with a bolded response on the next line — a defensible web rendering of Guía/Todos alternation. Note that the site's list omits several invocations of the current Vatican text (e.g. *Madre de la esperanza*, *Torre de marfil*, *Consuelo de los migrantes*, *Reina de la familia*) while keeping *Consolador de los Afligidos*, *Refugio de los pecadores* etc. [VAT-LIT]. See 5.2.

### 5.2 Textual variants (site vs Vatican/Mexican printed texts)

- **Letanías omissions**: site list lacks *Madre de la esperanza*, *Virgen digna de veneración/alabanza* (site has *Virgen Venerable*), *Torre de marfil*, *Consuelo de los migrantes*, *Consoladora de los afligidos* (site has singular *Consolador de los Afligidos*), *Reina de la familia*; site also uses *Madre de Jesucristo* (Vatican: *Madre de Cristo*) and *Madre de la divina Gracia* (capitalized oddly) [Letanías.astro vs VAT-LIT].
- **Mystery names**: site titles are close but compressed — e.g. site "El anuncio del Ángel a María" vs Vatican "La Encarnación del Hijo de Dios"; site "Su auto revelación en las bodas de Caná" vs "Las bodas de Caná"; site "El anuncio del Reino y llamado a la conversión" vs "El anuncio del Reino de Dios" [Misterios.tsx vs VAT-MIS-*]. These shorter Mexican-style formulations match several Mexican folk formulations; they are legitimate variants, not errors.
- **Gloriosos titles**: site "La coronación y glorificación de María" vs Vatican "La coronación de María como Reina y Señora de todo lo creado"; site "La asunción de María al Cielo" (lowercase) vs Vatican "La Asunción de María al cielo" [same].
- **Gozosos titles**: site "La visita de la Santísima Virgen a Santa Isabel" vs Vatican "La Visitación de Nuestra Señora a su prima Santa Isabel"; site "Jesús perdido y hallado en el templo" vs "El Niño Jesús perdido y hallado en el Templo" [same].
- **Señal de la Cruz**: site prints "libranos" (without accent) and "que estas en cielo" (missing accent and "l": should be "que estás en el cielo") in the Padre Nuestro [OracionInicial.astro; OracionIntencionesSantoPadre.astro; Jaculatorias.astro]. Accent/typo flags only.
- **Oración final**: the site's "Oración Final" begins with the **Cordero de Dios triptych** then *Bajo tu amparo* then the Rosary collect — a legitimate Mexican ordering [DLF-R ends with Letanía and the Litany's own collect; APM-PDF hands "las oraciones conclusivas" to a layperson]. The collect text on the site matches the Roman collect quoted by RVM §37 [VAT-RVM].
- **No Guadalupe-specific content at all**: the site never mentions the Virgen de Guadalupe, the jaculatoria "Santa María de Guadalupe, Reina de México", or any Guadalupe intention — despite the project's Guadalupe identity. This is the largest thematic gap vs Mexican practice, where even the generic Rosary is commonly closed with the Guadalupe versicle [APM-PDF].

### 5.3 Non-flags (confirmed correct)

- Weekday → mystery-set assignment (Mon/Sat gozosos, Thu luminosos, Tue/Fri dolorosos, Wed/Sun gloriosos) is exactly the RVM §38 scheme [Misterios.tsx vs VAT-RVM].
- The four mystery sets and their five members each are the correct mysteries; only titles are compressed (§5.2).
- The Act of Contrition text ("Señor mío Jesucristo, Dios y Hombre verdadero, creador y redentor mío…") is a standard traditional form [OracionInicial.astro; cf. APM-PDF's longer variant].
- The two opening jaculatorias ("Oh Soberano Santuario…" / "Emperatriz poderosa…") are traditional Mexican rosary verse [Jaculatorias.astro].

## Summary for the redesign

The site already carries the correct universal skeleton (mystery sets, weekday cycle, closing prayers). What the Guadalupe/Mexican tradition would add: (a) the **decade loop** as the structural spine — announcement, silence, Padrenuestro, 10 Avemarías, Gloria, Guadalupe jaculatoria; (b) the **Guía/Todos dialogue as rhythm**, which solo pray-ers simply read in full; (c) the **distinctive Mexican closing sequence** (Pope's intentions → 3 Avemarías fe/esperanza/caridad + Templo y Sagrario → Salve → Letanías); and (d) a **visual language drawn from the tilma-as-códice**: turquoise mantle, rose tunic, gold rays, stars, roses, the woven texture, and the Nahui Ollin four-petal motif — with "¿No estoy yo aquí, que soy tu Madre?" as the tradition's anchor sentence.
